Ollie / owner app

VISUAL DESIGN REFERENCE · iOS 17+ · SWIFTUI · SINGLE USER · MODE: PAPER

Ollie fires at most a handful of signals per trading day and each one dies in 15 minutes. The app therefore optimizes for two moments: the decision under a countdown, and the quiet check-in where nothing is pending. Everything else — positions, history, controls — is instrumentation around those two moments. The design language is a native iOS instrument: system type, hairline dividers, dense tabular data, and exactly one loud element — the mode rail.

System

Mode is chrome, not a badge

The single most important requirement: the owner must never wonder whether a tap moves real money. A per-screen badge gets tuned out; so instead the mode owns the app shell. A 22pt rail sits pinned directly under the status-bar clock on every screen (safeAreaInset(edge: .top) on the root TabView, above navigation), and the app's entire accent color derives from the mode. In paper, the tint is amber — the Approve button, the selected tab, the back chevron are amber because the app is simulating. Flipping to live would repaint every one of those surfaces crimson. The two rails also differ in texture (diagonal caution stripes vs. solid) and wording, so the distinction survives grayscale, color-blindness, and peripheral vision.

The two rails

LIVE · REAL ORDERS

Paper: striped amber, pencil glyph, "simulated fills." Live (Phase 5, currently impossible — LIVE_TRADING_ENABLED=false): solid crimson, record-dot glyph, "real orders." Stripes vs. solid is deliberate: even with color stripped, the shell looks different. The rail never scrolls away, never animates, never shows anything else.

Countdown states

08:42 04:12 01:07

Derived from expires_at, ticking via TimelineView(.periodic). Neutral above 5:00; amber at ≤5:00; red at ≤1:00 with a soft pulse (disabled under Reduce Motion). The depleting ring makes remaining fraction legible before the digits are read. SF Mono, tabular — the pill never changes width as it ticks.

Paper-amber is deliberately outside the gain/loss axis, so "simulation" never reads as "profit" or "danger." Green leans teal and red leans orange — the pair most separable under deutan and protan vision — and neither is ever the only carrier of meaning.

Money typography

Backend money is a decimal string of arbitrary precision. Rule: render verbatim, pad to two places minimum, and set everything past two decimals at 70% size in secondary color. Cents always visible, sub-cent precision preserved but de-weighted. All money is SF Pro with .monospacedDigit(), right-aligned in columns so magnitudes compare vertically.

P&L without hue

▲ +3.24 ▼ −2.08
▲ +3.24 ▼ −2.08

Same chips, grayscaled: sign and triangle still carry the direction. Every gain/loss value in the app ships with an explicit +/− and a ▲/▼; buy/sell chips carry the word itself. Color is reinforcement, never the message.

Type. Two voices, both native. SF Pro (system) for everything conversational — titles, theses, buttons. SF Mono for everything the machine measured — countdowns, indicator values, timestamps, quote ages. The mono voice marks "this is evidence, not prose," which is exactly the distinction the owner needs while deciding. Scale: 32 large title / 19–16 headings / 15 body / 13.5 secondary / 11 caption-caps with .1em tracking.

The queue, and the state it's usually in

Everything needed to triage is on the card's first line — symbol, side, size, estimated price, time left — with the thesis as a two-line teaser underneath. Both signals here came from the same 9:35 scan, so their countdowns agree; that's honest, not lazy. The third frame is the screen the owner actually sees most days: empty is the default state, and it's designed as a heartbeat, not an absence.

Approvals · empty · the most common state — a heartbeat, not an absence
Triage in one lineSymbol, side, size × price, and time remaining share the card's first row. The owner can rank two signals against each other before reading a word of thesis.
Side is triple-encodedBUY/SELL chips carry the word, a ▲/▼ glyph, and a tint — never tint alone. Sell rows are not "errors"; the chip tint is quiet.
The fallback markerWhen thesis_source = fallback_template, a small outlined TEMPLATE chip with a document glyph sits at the start of the meta row. Grey, not amber — it means "a rule wrote this sentence, weigh it accordingly," not "something is wrong." LLM-written theses carry no marker; absence is the normal state.
Broker alerts surface earlyA signal whose review_snapshot carries alerts shows an amber "1 broker alert" flag in the list, so the owner opens that one knowing there's something to read.
The empty state is the productMost days the queue is empty, so the screen reports why it's empty: scanner running, last scan and its result, next scan, kill switch off. Silence becomes evidence the system is alive — and if the kill switch were on, this is where it would confront the owner.
Screen 2 — Signal detail · the decision

Fast to read, hard to fat-finger

The two frames are the same signal at two moments. At 9:46 the countdown band has turned amber; at 9:49 the owner has tapped Approve… and the confirmation sheet is up — with the countdown still ticking inside it, now red. Approve and Reject are single taps followed by exactly one sheet; the trailing ellipsis is the iOS convention for "a confirmation follows."

Countdown gets a band, not a pillOn the decision screen the countdown earns 28pt mono digits inside its own card with a depleting track — glanceable from arm's length. The band adopts the threshold color whole, so peripheral vision reads urgency before the digits do.
Alerts sit above the thesisreview_snapshot alerts render as mono chips with a plain-English gloss, placed between the countdown and the thesis. The prose argument never outranks the broker's objection.

The sheet can't hide the clockThe countdown repeats inside the sheet in its critical state. If it expires mid-sheet, the button disables and the sheet reports "Expired — window closed" rather than failing on tap.
Screen 3 — Decided history

Three outcomes, three temperatures

Every signal leaves pending exactly once, with a reason attached — the history is an immutable ledger, and it reads like one. Approved is teal with its fill price. Rejected is deliberately gray: saying no is a valid outcome, not a failure. Expired is amber and worded as a miss — "no decision within 15:00" — because the system worked; the owner just wasn't there.

Screen 4 — Dashboard · positions

Live numbers, and the honest version without them

Two frames, two truth states. Left: broker reachable, quotes 12 seconds old, full P&L. Right: the common degraded state — broker unreachable, so every quote-derived number is withheld, not zeroed and not guessed. Entry basis stays; dashes mark what's missing; the banner says when data was last good. And realized P&L: 0.00 is framed as a fact of the system's phase ("no closed lots yet"), so a permanent zero never looks like a broken query.

Screen 5 — Controls

One big red button, one lock that isn't yours to open

The kill switch is the only red control surface in the app — nothing competes with it. The paper/live segmented control renders locked because LIVE_TRADING_ENABLED=false is set at the environment level: the UI shows the lock and says plainly that enabling live is a deploy, not a tap. Showing a disabled control (instead of hiding it) documents the safety model on the device itself.

Halt gets the ellipsis too"Halt Ollie…" opens one destructive confirmation sheet (red button, octagon icon) listing exactly what happens: pipeline stops, pending signals expire, nothing fires until re-enabled. Same one-step pattern as Approve — muscle memory transfers.
After the haltThe Controls card flips to a "Halted" state with an amber "Resume…" action, and the Approvals empty state reports "Kill switch: On" — the stop is visible from the screen the owner lands on, not just from Settings.
The locked segment is documentationLive is shown, locked, with the env var named. Hiding it would invite the question "where do I go live?"; a lock with a reason answers it and reinforces that the gate is outside the phone's authority.
Service card = trustCron schedule, sweep cadence, heartbeat age, broker session. The same vocabulary as the empty state's heartbeat card, so "is it running?" has the same answer everywhere.

SwiftUI build notes

Mode rail — root TabView wrapped with .safeAreaInset(edge: .top); stripes via a tiled Canvas. App-wide .tint(mode.accent) drives buttons, tabs, links from one token.
Countdown — TimelineView(.periodic(from:by: 0.5)) over expires_at; digits .monospacedDigit(); ring is Circle().trim(from:to:). Pulse gated on accessibilityReduceMotion; VoiceOver reads "4 minutes 12 seconds remaining."
Money — keep the backend decimal string; split on the decimal point, render past-2 digits as a smaller Text in .secondary. Never round, never Float.
Confirmations — .sheet with presentationDetents([.medium]) for approve/reject/halt; a summary list, one filled button naming verb + mode, Cancel below.
Iconography — SF Symbols: tray.full, chart.xyaxis.line, clock, slider.horizontal.3, arrowtriangle.up/down.fill, exclamationmark.triangle, doc.plaintext (template thesis), lock.fill, hourglass, checkmark, xmark, octagon (halt), pencil.line (paper).
Color assets — every token above ships as an asset-catalog color with dark/light variants; gain/loss additionally pass a grayscale legibility check (sign + triangle always present).
